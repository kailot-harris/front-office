# WNBA leader reliability study (issue #267)

Research only. This study measures how quickly each league-leader stat settles as a player's sample grows. It repeats the NBA study (NBA_analytics#253) for the WNBA. It does not choose any qualifying minimum and changes no production code; the owner reviews these curves and sets the minimums, which the WNBA leaders panel then uses.

Generated by `tools/leader_reliability_study.R` (seed 267, 1000 bootstrap resamples). Runtime of this run: 1.4 minutes.

## Data

- WNBA regular seasons 2005, 2006, 2007, 2008, 2009, 2010, 2011, 2012, 2013, 2014, 2015, 2016, 2017, 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 (22 seasons, 3,589 player-seasons). Every stat below, counting, shooting and rates alike, uses all of these seasons: no box-score field is missing in any of them (see the table). 2003 and 2004 are left out because wehoop's ESPN player box covers only 77% and 90% of their scheduled games (the rule is at least 95%); 2002 has no wehoop team box; 2027 has not started. 2020 is included: it was a complete 22-game regular season played at a single site.
- Season lengths differ: 34 games per team in 2005-2019, 22 in 2020, 32 in 2021, 36 in 2022, 40 in 2023-2024 and 44 in 2025-2026. Older seasons therefore contribute fewer games per player-season, and the largest game counts on the grid come mostly from recent seasons.
- Player and team box scores: ESPN via wehoop (`load_wnba_player_box`, `load_wnba_team_box`). Regular season means ESPN `season_type == 2` and schedule type `STD` (`load_wnba_schedule`), which removes the All-Star game and the Commissioner's Cup final; neither counts in the standings or the season statistics.
- Rows flagged `did_not_play` are excluded. Rows not flagged but with no minutes recorded are excluded and counted below; before 2013 ESPN did not flag players who did not play. Across all seasons 0 of these rows have any points scored (`minutes_missing_rows_with_points` in the JSON), so they are players who did not play rather than lost minutes. Nothing is imputed: a missing value makes any sample that contains it missing at that point, and the number of such player-seasons is reported at every point (`n_missing` in the JSON).
- A player-season covers every team the player played for that season. Team context (team minutes, team and opponent totals) is taken game by game for the team she played for in that game.

## Methods

- **Split-half reliability.** Each player-season's games are numbered in date order; odd-numbered games form half A and even-numbered games half B. At each sample size n the stat is computed on each half from the first n units of that half, for every player-season whose two halves each reach n units, and the two halves are correlated across player-seasons (Pearson). The raw correlation is the reliability of an n-unit sample. The Spearman-Brown value 2r / (1 + r) is the projected reliability of a 2n-unit sample and is plotted at 2n.
- **Rest-of-season check.** The stat over a player's first n units of the season against the same stat over all of her remaining games that season. Rule: only player-seasons whose remaining games hold at least n units are used, so the remainder is never smaller than the early sample.
- **Units.** Counting stats: games played. Shooting: attempts of that shot type. Rates: minutes. Games are indivisible, so for attempts and minutes a sample of n is the shortest run of whole games whose cumulative units reach n; the mean realised size is reported in the JSON (`mean_realised_units`).
- **Intervals.** Player-season block bootstrap: player-seasons are resampled with replacement (1000 resamples, seed 267), every curve point is recomputed on each resample with the same draw, and the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles are reported. Points with fewer than 30 player-seasons are not reported.
- **Share of season.** Every crossing is also shown as a share of a 44-game season (the current WNBA schedule). For games that is n / 44. For attempts and minutes it is n over a full season for a typical rotation player: 44 games times the median per-game value among rotation player-seasons (those that played in at least half of the most games any player played that season), pooled over all seasons used:

| Unit | Median per game (rotation player-seasons) | Full-season reference (x season games) |
|---|---|---|
| games | 1.0 | 44 |
| field goal attempts | 6.2 | 274 |
| 3-point attempts | 1.5 | 67 |
| free throw attempts | 1.5 | 68 |
| minutes | 21.1 | 928 |

- **Reading the curves at large n.** Only high-volume players reach large samples, so the player pool narrows as n grows; a narrower spread of true talent lowers correlations. A curve that flattens or dips at the right-hand end reflects that selection as well as noise, and the player count at each point should be read with it. WNBA seasons are short, so the pool narrows sooner than in the NBA study.
- **Who is in the pool.** As issue #267 specifies, every player-season that reaches n is correlated, from bench players to stars. The spread of true levels across that pool is wide (a starter's points per game differ from a reserve's mostly through role and minutes), and a wide spread makes correlations high at small n. Separating players near the top of a leader list from each other is harder than these curves suggest, so the crossings below describe the whole league, not the top of a list.

## Formulas

Counting stats are totals over the sample divided by games played. Shooting percentages are made over attempted for that shot type: field goal % = FG / FGA, 3-point % = 3P / 3PA, free throw % = FT / FTA.

Rates use Basketball Reference definitions, computed from player and team box scores. For a sample of games each rate is the sum of its per-game numerators over the sum of its per-game denominators. MP is the player's minutes; TmMP is the sum of her team's player minutes in that game; Tm and Opp are the team box totals of her team and its opponent in that game. Team turnovers (TmTOV) are the sum of the team's player turnovers in that game, for every season. The team box `turnovers` column is not used because its meaning changes: from 2013 it always equals the player sum, but before 2013 it does so in only 40-62% of team-games (last column of the data table).

| Rate | Formula |
|---|---|
| Effective field goal % | (FG + 0.5 x 3P) / FGA |
| True shooting % | PTS / (2 x (FGA + 0.44 x FTA)) |
| Turnover rate | 100 x TOV / (FGA + 0.44 x FTA + TOV) |
| Offensive rebound rate | 100 x ORB x (TmMP / 5) / (MP x (TmORB + OppDRB)) |
| Defensive rebound rate | 100 x DRB x (TmMP / 5) / (MP x (TmDRB + OppORB)) |
| Assist rate | 100 x AST / (((MP / (TmMP / 5)) x TmFG) - FG) |
| Usage rate | 100 x ((FGA + 0.44 x FTA + TOV) x (TmMP / 5)) / (MP x (TmFGA + 0.44 x TmFTA + TmTOV)) |

RAPM (offensive, defensive, total) is the possession-level ridge regression in `rapm_engine.R`, in points per 100 possessions; see the RAPM section.

## RAPM

Seasons fit: 2005, 2006, 2007, 2008, 2009, 2010, 2011, 2012, 2013, 2014, 2015, 2016, 2017, 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026. Data: `wehoop::load_wnba_stats_possessions()` (the source `rapm_engine.R` uses), one row per possession with both five-player lineups. For each season the regular-season games (WNBA Stats game ids with prefix `102`; this excludes the playoffs and the Commissioner's Cup final) are sorted by game id, which follows the schedule, and alternately assigned to an odd half and an even half. `rapm_engine.R` is sourced unchanged; its `build_possession_design_matrix()` (which drops possessions without a full lineup or with an impossible points value, as in production) and `solve_ridge_rapm()` (lambda = 900, the engine's `RAPM_LAMBDA`) are fit on each half separately, with the same per-100 scaling, defensive sign flip and per-side centring as `fit_rapm_ratings()`. Players rated in both halves are paired. A player-season's exposure is the smallest of her offensive and defensive possessions across the two halves ("possessions per side in each half").

### Comparison with the current rule (`RAPM_LEADERBOARD_MIN_POSS_PER_SIDE` = 1,000 possessions each side, `config.R`)

The production leaderboard fits one pooled RAPM over seasons 2019-2026 and marks a player `leaderboard_eligible` when she has at least 1,000 possessions on both offence and defence in that pooled fit. This study fits one season at a time, so the comparison is made at the season scale: a season-scale exposure of 1,000 per side corresponds to about 500 per side in each half, and the Spearman-Brown value projects the half-season correlation to the full season. In the pooled production fit a player can reach the rule's count by adding up possessions over several seasons, and her rating there draws on all of them, so the rule is not the same thing as a single-season count; these single-season figures describe what that count of possessions buys within one season.

At the rule's count as a season-scale exposure (half of it in each half):

- orapm: split-half r 0.33 [0.28, 0.38] among the 1482 player-seasons with at least 500 per side in each half; Spearman-Brown (season scale, about 1,000 per side) 0.50 [0.44, 0.55].
- drapm: split-half r 0.18 [0.13, 0.24] among the 1482 player-seasons with at least 500 per side in each half; Spearman-Brown (season scale, about 1,000 per side) 0.31 [0.23, 0.38].
- total_rapm: split-half r 0.34 [0.29, 0.38] among the 1482 player-seasons with at least 500 per side in each half; Spearman-Brown (season scale, about 1,000 per side) 0.50 [0.45, 0.55].

At the rule's count reached within each half (about twice the rule over a season):

- orapm: split-half r 0.21 [0.05, 0.37] among the 128 player-seasons with at least 1,000 per side in each half; Spearman-Brown (season scale, about 2,000 per side) 0.35 [0.10, 0.54].
- drapm: split-half r 0.31 [0.15, 0.47] among the 128 player-seasons with at least 1,000 per side in each half; Spearman-Brown (season scale, about 2,000 per side) 0.48 [0.27, 0.64].
- total_rapm: split-half r 0.33 [0.17, 0.46] among the 128 player-seasons with at least 1,000 per side in each half; Spearman-Brown (season scale, about 2,000 per side) 0.50 [0.29, 0.63].

Caveats specific to RAPM: ridge shrinkage pulls low-exposure players toward 0 in both halves, and half-season fits carry the same penalty as the production fit on a small fraction of its data, so these correlations describe the engine as configured, not an unshrunk rating. The bootstrap resamples player-seasons from the fitted pairs; it does not refit the ridge, so its intervals omit fitting uncertainty. Players in the same lineups share possessions, so ratings are not independent across players.

## Reference points for the owner's review

For each stat, the first grid sample size at which each curve reaches the bar, with a bootstrap 95% interval for that crossing and, in parentheses, the point value as a share of a 44-game season (see Methods). "Beyond grid" means some resamples did not reach the bar inside the grid. These are reference points for review, not chosen values. Split-half and rest-of-season values are in the stat's own unit for an n-unit sample; Spearman-Brown values are the projected sample (2n) at which reliability reaches the bar. The pool is every player-season, from bench players to stars, so separating players near the top of a leader list is harder than these crossings suggest.
