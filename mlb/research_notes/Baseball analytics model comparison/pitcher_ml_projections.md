# ML/Deep Learning Applied to MLB Pitcher Performance Projection (ERA, FIP, K%, BB%, WHIP)

## What published papers/writeups use ML/gradient boosting to project MLB pitching stats (ERA, FIP, etc.)?

### Takeaway
Most rigorous, citable work applying gradient boosting/ensemble ML specifically to pitcher counting/rate stats (ERA, WHIP, K%, BB%) comes from technical blogs (Towards Data Science/Medium, personal analytics blogs) rather than peer-reviewed venues; the closest peer-reviewed analogue is a 2026 KBO (Korean league, not MLB) journal paper using XGBoost/LightGBM/CatBoost on ERA/WHIP/BB/SO, and a 2022 arXiv LSTM paper on MLB hitters (home runs, not pitching stats). No peer-reviewed MLB-specific pitcher ERA/FIP XGBoost paper with reported RMSE was found — this is a notable gap.

### Cited Findings
- "Baseball and Machine Learning Part 2: A Data Science Approach to 2021 Pitching Projections" (Towards Data Science/Medium) used XGBoost regression trained on 2016–2019 MLB data to predict 11 separate pitching statistics via separate models each: IP, W, L, SV, BS, HLD, HRA, K, ERA, WHIP, and FanGraphs' Dollar value (Dol); starters were filtered to a 60-IP floor and relievers to a 40-IP floor — [TDS article](https://towardsdatascience.com/baseball-and-machine-learning-part-2-a-data-science-approach-to-2021-pitching-projections-530dbfe6dcc4/)
- The companion piece "Baseball and Machine Learning: A Data Science Approach to 2021 Hitting Projections" exists for hitters using the same author's XGBoost approach (noted for context, not pitching) — [Medium](https://medium.com/data-science/baseball-and-machine-learning-a-data-science-approach-to-2021-hitting-projections-4d6eeed01ede)
- A 2026 paper in the Journal of Information and Communication Convergence Engineering (Kim, Kim, Jung, Lee, Vol. 24(1)) compared CatBoost, LightGBM, and XGBoost for KBO (Korea Baseball Organization, NOT MLB) player performance prediction, with pitching modeled via 30 features including ERA, WHIP, BB, SO; trained on 2009–2023 seasons, validated against actual 2024 season results, using 5-fold cross-validation for hyperparameter tuning; reported metrics were RMSE, MAE, R², and Spearman's rank correlation, though the only R²/correlation figures extracted were for batting (R²=0.933, Spearman=0.973) — pitching-specific accuracy figures were not retrievable from the fetched summary — [JICCE](https://www.jicce.org/journal/view.html?doi=10.56977%2Fjicce.2026.24.1.18)
- "Machine Learning in Baseball Analytics: Sabermetrics and Beyond" (MDPI, journal *Information*, vol 16(5), 2025/2026) is a broader review on ML in baseball sabermetrics but its full content could not be retrieved (403 Forbidden on fetch) — only the title/venue is confirmed via search snippet — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2078-2489/16/5/361)
- "Predicting Baseball Players' Value through Machine Learning: 'Traditional metrics' vs 'Advanced metrics'" appears in search results as comparing ML approaches using traditional vs. advanced (Statcast-like) metrics, but full text was not fetched/confirmed — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/382249191_Predicting_Baseball_Players'_Value_through_Machine_Learning_Traditional_metrics_vs_Advanced_metrics)
- statshacker.com (personal analytics blog, author "statshacker") published "Prediction of Strikeout Rate (K%) by Machine Learning" (June 17, 2018) using a small feedforward neural network (architecture n_x–5–5–1, two hidden layers of 5 units, softplus activation, logistic output) to predict strikeout outcomes from three inputs: batter K%, pitcher K%, and league K% (seasonal). Validation used k=28 k-fold cross-validation (one season per fold) plus an 80/20 train/test split (training further split 64%/16% for weight-fitting/early-stopping). For 2017 the reported metrics were Cross Entropy = 0.500(3), Brier Score = 0.162, AUROC = 0.640 — [statshacker.com](http://statshacker.com/blog/2018/06/17/prediction-of-strikeout-rate-k-by-machine-learning/)

### Inferences
- The field is dominated by independent/hobbyist blog analyses (TDS, Medium, personal blogs) applying off-the-shelf gradient boosting (XGBoost) to classic box-score pitching stats, rather than peer-reviewed academic validation with formal RMSE/MAE benchmarking against established systems.
- Where peer-reviewed gradient-boosting work exists, it is increasingly appearing for international leagues (KBO) rather than MLB specifically, suggesting MLB-specific peer-reviewed ERA/FIP ML projection papers are rare or non-existent as of this research.

## What algorithms are used?

### Takeaway
Across the identified sources, algorithm use splits into: (1) gradient boosting (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost) for tabular box-score/Statcast stat prediction; (2) small feedforward neural networks for binary/probabilistic outcome prediction (e.g., K% matchups); (3) LSTM/sequence models for career-trajectory and plate-appearance-level prediction; (4) random forests for pitch-type/pitcher-specific game-level predictions; (5) emerging transformer-based/NLP-style sequence embeddings for in-game player impact.

### Cited Findings
- XGBoost regression was used for the TDS 2021 pitching projections (11 separate single-target models) — [TDS](https://towardsdatascience.com/baseball-and-machine-learning-part-2-a-data-science-approach-to-2021-pitching-projections-530dbfe6dcc4/)
- The KBO 2026 JICCE paper compared CatBoost, LightGBM, and XGBoost directly against each other for batting, fielding, and pitching prediction, ultimately selecting XGBoost as the best overall performer — [JICCE](https://www.jicce.org/journal/view.html?doi=10.56977%2Fjicce.2026.24.1.18)
- A 2022 arXiv paper (Sun, Lin, Tsai; arXiv:2206.09654, "Performance Prediction in Major League Baseball by Long Short-Term Memory Networks") used LSTM networks to predict home runs (a hitting stat, not pitching) and compared LSTM against "several machine learning models and a widely used baseball projection system, sZymborski [ZiPS] Projection System," reporting LSTM "has better performance than others and has the ability to make more exact predictions" — specific quantitative metrics (RMSE/MAE) were not retrievable from the abstract-level fetch — [arXiv 2206.09654](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.09654)
- A small feedforward neural network (2 hidden layers of 5 nodes, softplus/logistic activations) was used by statshacker.com for K% prediction — [statshacker.com](http://statshacker.com/blog/2018/06/17/prediction-of-strikeout-rate-k-by-machine-learning/)
- Random forest models are used in practice for pitcher-specific, pitch-level and game-level prediction systems per GitHub project descriptions, e.g. "pitcher-specific Random Forest trained on historical Statcast data and evaluated chronologically against future games," and ensemble daily-prediction systems combining XGBoost, LightGBM, Random Forest, and Logistic Regression to predict strikeout totals — these are informal/non-peer-reviewed GitHub repos, not published papers, so should be treated as illustrative of practitioner methodology rather than validated research — [GitHub repos via search snippet, exact repos not individually confirmed]
- Penn State researchers (Connor Heaton, doctoral candidate, and Prasenjit Mitra, professor, College of Information Sciences and Technology, 2022) developed an NLP/computer-vision-inspired sequential modeling approach that embeds baseball game events (not pitcher-specific, covers both pitchers and batters) into 64-element vectors; features included 325 game-state changes (ball-strike counts, base occupancy), pitch-by-pitch data (pitch type, launch angle), and season-level stats (WHIP for pitchers, OPS for batters); when combined with traditional sabermetrics, the embeddings helped predict game winners with "over 59% accuracy" — this is a general player-impact model, not a direct ERA/FIP projection system — [ScienceDaily summary of Penn State research](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2022/04/220406101805.htm)
- Ganeshapillai & Guttag (MIT), "A data-driven method for in-game decision making in MLB: when to pull a starting pitcher," presented at KDD 2013, used regularized linear regression (not a tree ensemble or deep net) to build pitcher-specific models predicting whether a starting pitcher would surrender a run in the next inning if left in, using MLB data from 2006–2010; their model disagreed with actual manager decisions 48% of the time and showed improved accuracy over naive "manager" baseline predictions — this predicts an in-game binary outcome, not season-long ERA/FIP — [Semantic Scholar/ACM via search](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2487575.2487660)

### Inferences
- Gradient boosting (XGBoost specifically) is the most commonly chosen algorithm in both blog-level and the one peer-reviewed (KBO) tabular-stat projection work, likely due to its strong out-of-box performance on structured/tabular sports statistics.
- Deep learning (LSTM, NLP-style embeddings) is being explored mainly for sequence-dependent problems (career trajectories, in-game event sequences) rather than direct season-ending rate-stat regression (ERA, FIP, WHIP), where simpler tree-based models remain dominant.

### Gaps
- No RNN/LSTM or transformer architecture specifically targeting pitcher ERA/FIP/WHIP/K%/BB% projection (as opposed to home runs or game-outcome prediction) was found in this search pass.
- Could not verify architecture details or quantitative results of the "Temporal Fusion Transformer" paper titled "Pitcher Performance Prediction Major League Baseball (MLB) by Temporal Fusion Transformer" — ResearchGate page returned 403 Forbidden on fetch; only the title (confirming a transformer-based pitcher-specific MLB prediction paper exists) could be confirmed via search snippet — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/391148375_Pitcher_Performance_Prediction_Major_League_Baseball_MLB_by_Temporal_Fusion_Transformer). This is a promising lead for a dedicated MLB pitcher deep-learning paper that could not be fully investigated — flagged as a priority follow-up.

## What features are used — traditional box-score stats only, or Statcast/pitch-tracking features?

### Takeaway
Feature sets vary widely: the TDS XGBoost pitching model appears to rely on traditional rolling box-score stats (IP, ERA, WHIP, counting stats) rather than Statcast; the KBO JICCE paper explicitly uses traditional box-score-style features (30 features: ERA, WHIP, BB, SO, etc.) rather than pitch-tracking; by contrast, several non-MLB-season-stat projects (whiff/injury-risk focused) explicitly incorporate Statcast pitch-tracking data (velocity, spin rate, movement, release point, pose/kinematic data from video).

### Cited Findings
- The TDS 2021 pitching projection model's predictors were not explicitly itemized in retrievable content beyond the trained target stats themselves (IP, W, L, SV, BS, HLD, HRA, K, ERA, WHIP, Dol) using 2016-2019 historical seasonal data — suggesting traditional rolling box-score/seasonal inputs rather than Statcast pitch-tracking — [TDS](https://towardsdatascience.com/baseball-and-machine-learning-part-2-a-data-science-approach-to-2021-pitching-projections-530dbfe6dcc4/)
- The KBO JICCE 2026 paper used 30 traditional pitching features (ERA, WHIP, BB, SO, etc.) — no Statcast/pitch-tracking inputs were mentioned — [JICCE](https://www.jicce.org/journal/view.html?doi=10.56977%2Fjicce.2026.24.1.18)
- GitHub project "brandongrewal77/mlb-whiff-prediction" predicts MLB swing-and-miss outcomes using Statcast pitch characteristics (velocity, movement, location, pitch type, handedness, count) with random forest classification — an informal, non-peer-reviewed project, but illustrative of Statcast-feature usage for pitch-level (not season-stat) outcomes — [GitHub](https://github.com/brandongrewal77/mlb-whiff-prediction)
- GitHub project "yasumorishima/baseball-mlops" explicitly combines Statcast features with LightGBM/CatBoost/Bayesian models and benchmarks against the Marcel projection system — a non-peer-reviewed open-source project but directly relevant as an example of Statcast-vs-Marcel comparison methodology — [GitHub](https://github.com/yasumorishima/baseball-mlops)
- The 2026 arXiv paper "Scalable Injury-Risk Screening in Baseball Pitching From Broadcast Video" (Bright, Mende, Zelek) uses computer-vision-derived pose/kinematic features extracted from standard broadcast video (not dedicated motion-capture or radar/Statcast hardware) to screen for injury-risk pitching biomechanics, building on prior "PitcherNet" and "DREAM" pose-estimation models by the same lead author — [arXiv 2603.04864](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.04864)
- A study referenced in search results used "pitch-tracking metrics... as predictors of future shoulder and elbow injuries in MLB pitchers using machine learning and game-theory based approaches" — title/authors not independently confirmed via fetch, found only via search snippet.
- An "emerginginvestigators.org" paper, "Predicting Baseball Pitcher Efficacy Using Physical Pitch Characteristics," appears (via search snippet only — PDF fetch failed, unreadable binary) to use 16 game-independent pitch-characteristic features to predict efficacy metrics including WHIP, batting average against (BAA), and FIP via neural network models — this is a student/research-archive publication (emerginginvestigators.org is a peer-reviewed journal for high-school/early researchers), so credibility and rigor are lower than professional academic venues; findings could not be verified beyond the search-engine-generated summary — [emerginginvestigators.org](https://emerginginvestigators.org/articles/22-270/pdf) (unverified — flagged as low confidence)

### Inferences
- There appears to be a split in the literature: season-long rate-stat projection (ERA, WHIP, FIP as annual aggregates) tends to rely on traditional box-score/seasonal rolling stats as inputs, while pitch-level or short-horizon prediction (whiff probability, next-inning run probability, injury risk) is where Statcast/pitch-tracking and now video-based kinematic features are being actively used.
- This suggests Statcast integration into full-season ERA/FIP-style projection models (as opposed to pitch-level classification) is relatively underexplored in the literature surfaced here — most Statcast-heavy work targets injury risk, whiff/strikeout probability, or pitch-type classification rather than full rate-stat season projections.

### Gaps
- Could not find a rigorous, peer-reviewed study that directly quantifies accuracy improvement from adding Statcast/pitch-tracking features versus a box-score-only baseline for season-long ERA/FIP/WHIP/K%/BB% projection specifically (see next section — this is the central quantitative gap in this research).
- The "emerginginvestigators.org" findings and the pitch-tracking-injury study could not be verified with direct source fetches; both are noted as low-confidence/unverified.

## What is each system validated against, and what accuracy is reported? Does Statcast data measurably improve projection accuracy vs. box-score-only models?

### Takeaway
Quantified, citable evidence that Statcast/pitch-tracking features measurably improve ERA/FIP/WHIP projection accuracy over box-score-only models was NOT found in this research pass — this is the most significant gap relative to the research objective. The only concrete comparative accuracy figures retrieved relate to (a) historical projection-system accuracy (ZiPS/PECOTA/Marcel RMSE on ERA, not ML-specific) and (b) the KBO batting R²/Spearman figures (not pitching, not MLB).

### Cited Findings
- Historical (non-ML) projection system comparison from Beyond the Box Score: over the 2006–2011 period, measuring RMSE of ERA predictions, ZiPS ranked best (RMSE 1.143), followed by PECOTA (1.141) and Marcel (1.132); despite this, Marcel's simpler methodology "topped PECOTA and ZiPS... due to having a far lower standard deviation," and for pitchers specifically "ZiPS struggled... and was the least accurate in all four categories, often by large margins" in a separate single-season grading — [Beyond the Box Score, "Grading the projections: 2016, part two"](https://www.beyondtheboxscore.com/2017/1/25/14366728/pecota-zips-steamer-marcel-projecting-pitchers-graded) and [A guide to the projection systems](https://www.beyondtheboxscore.com/2016/2/22/11079186/projections-marcel-pecota-zips-steamer-explained-guide-math-is-fun)
- Beyond the Box Score also notes: "More complex systems like ZiPS, Steamer, and PECOTA layer in pitch-level data, minor league translations, and machine learning, but they only marginally outperform Marcel on aggregate accuracy" — this is a directly relevant, if qualitative, finding suggesting that added data complexity (including pitch-level/Statcast-style data) yields only marginal gains over simple baseline systems for pitcher projection — [Beyond the Box Score](https://www.beyondtheboxscore.com/2016/2/22/11079186/projections-marcel-pecota-zips-steamer-explained-guide-math-is-fun)
- The KBO JICCE 2026 paper reported R²=0.933 and Spearman correlation=0.973 for XGBoost on batting predictions (pitching-specific R²/correlation values were not retrievable from the fetched summary, only that pitching used 30 features including ERA/WHIP/BB/SO) — [JICCE](https://www.jicce.org/journal/view.html?doi=10.56977%2Fjicce.2026.24.1.18)
- statshacker's K%-prediction neural network reported, for 2017: Cross Entropy 0.500(3), Brier Score 0.162, AUROC 0.640 — these are probabilistic/classification-style metrics (not RMSE/MAE on a continuous K% value), validated via 28-fold (one-season-per-fold) cross-validation — [statshacker.com](http://statshacker.com/blog/2018/06/17/prediction-of-strikeout-rate-k-by-botston/) [statshacker.com](http://statshacker.com/blog/2018/06/17/prediction-of-strikeout-rate-k-by-machine-learning/)
- The open-source "yasumorishima/baseball-mlops" project explicitly describes itself as comparing Statcast-feature-based LightGBM/CatBoost/Bayesian models "with Marcel" — i.e., an explicit ML-vs-traditional-system benchmark exists in the practitioner/open-source space, but as an unpublished GitHub repo (not peer-reviewed or blog-documented with reported numbers in the retrievable snippet), no quantitative results could be confirmed — [GitHub](https://github.com/yasumorishima/baseball-mlops) (unverified accuracy — flagged as a lead for follow-up, not a citable result)

### Inferences
- The qualitative finding that "machine learning" layering (as used by Steamer/ZiPS/PECOTA) produces only "marginal" aggregate accuracy gains over the simple Marcel baseline is the closest available evidence to answering whether advanced data/methods help — and it suggests gains are modest rather than dramatic, at least for these established hybrid systems circa the mid-2010s.
- Lack of any clearly sourced head-to-head "box-score-only XGBoost" vs. "Statcast-enhanced XGBoost" RMSE comparison for ERA/FIP indicates this specific quantitative question is either answered only in non-public/paywalled industry work (e.g., team proprietary models) or in sources not surfaced by this search.

- Pitching-specific R²/RMSE/correlation figures from the KBO JICCE 2026 paper could not be retrieved (only batting figures were visible in the fetched summary) — a follow-up fetch of the full paper PDF would be needed.

## Is there work on predicting injury risk or pitcher decline/aging curves using ML that affects projection systems?

### Takeaway
Confirmed academic work exists on ML-based UCL (elbow ligament) injury prediction and, more recently (2026), scalable computer-vision-based injury-risk screening from broadcast video — both using biomechanical/kinematic rather than classic box-score features — but no direct evidence was found connecting these injury models to adjustments in mainstream rate-stat projection systems (i.e., no confirmed paper shows injury-risk ML output being fed into ERA/FIP projections).

### Cited Findings
- "Predicting Ulnar Collateral Ligament Injury in Rookie Major League Baseball Pitchers" (arXiv, exact authors/year not independently confirmed beyond search listing) applies ML to predict UCL (Tommy John) injury risk specifically in rookie MLB pitchers — [arXiv 2207.00585](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.00585) (title/topic confirmed via search listing; full text not fetched in this pass)
- "Scalable Injury-Risk Screening in Baseball Pitching From Broadcast Video" by Jerrin Bright, Justin Mende, and John Zelek (arXiv:2603.04864, dated 2026) develops a computer-vision system using pose estimation/skeletal tracking from standard broadcast video (no specialized motion-capture hardware required) to screen for injury-risk pitching biomechanics, building on the same lead author's prior "PitcherNet" and "DREAM" pose-estimation models; the paper references validation against "established biomechanical injury risk assessments" drawing on prior kinematics-injury correlation studies, but specific quantitative accuracy metrics were not extractable from the fetched content — [arXiv 2603.04864](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.04864)
- A separate study (title/authors not confirmed, found via search snippet only) reportedly used "pitch-tracking metrics... as predictors of future shoulder and elbow injuries in MLB pitchers using machine learning and game-theory based approaches" — unverified beyond snippet.
- "Understanding Career Progression in Baseball Through Machine Learning" (arXiv:1712.05754) was surfaced in search results as potentially relevant to aging-curve/career-trajectory modeling, but full text was not fetched in this pass to confirm whether it covers pitchers specifically or decline curves — flagged for follow-up.

### Inferences
- Injury-risk ML research for pitchers is trending toward lower-cost, broadly-deployable computer-vision approaches (using existing broadcast video rather than lab/Statcast-grade motion capture), which could eventually make injury-adjusted projections more feasible/scalable across the league, but this integration step was not found as an already-published, validated system in the sources reviewed.

### Gaps
- No source was found that explicitly combines an injury-risk ML model's output with a traditional or ML-based rate-stat (ERA/FIP/WHIP) projection system — i.e., no evidence of an "injury-adjusted" ERA/FIP projection system using ML in the literature surfaced.
- Full text and quantitative accuracy metrics for both the UCL-injury-prediction paper (arXiv 2207.00585) and the broadcast-video injury-risk screening paper (arXiv 2603.04864) were not fully retrieved (metadata/abstract-level detail only for the latter; the former was not fetched at all in this pass) — a follow-up direct fetch of these two arXiv abstracts/PDFs is recommended if deeper detail is needed.
- Could not confirm whether "Understanding Career Progression in Baseball Through Machine Learning" (arXiv:1712.05754) addresses pitcher aging curves/decline specifically, or is hitter-focused — full text not fetched.
