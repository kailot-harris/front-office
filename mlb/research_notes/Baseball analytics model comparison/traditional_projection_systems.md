# Traditional MLB Projection Systems: Methodology and Accuracy Comparisons (Marcel, PECOTA, ZiPS, Steamer, THE BAT/THE BAT X)

## Which papers or rigorous writeups directly compare projection accuracy (RMSE, MAE, correlation) across two or more systems?

### Takeaway
There is no peer-reviewed, JQAS-caliber paper that directly benchmarks Marcel vs. PECOTA vs. ZiPS vs. Steamer vs. THE BAT on RMSE/MAE/correlation for wOBA, OBP, SLG, ERA, or FIP. The rigorous head-to-head accuracy comparisons that exist are all non-peer-reviewed sabermetric writeups (FanGraphs/THT/Beyond the Box Score/Baseball Prospectus/tangotiger.com), several of which use formal statistical methodology (RMSE, MAE/AAE, Pearson correlation, paired t-tests). One JQAS paper (Gerber & Craig 2021) benchmarks a new statistical model against Marcels specifically, which is the closest true academic comparison found.

### Cited Findings
- Matt Swartz (FanGraphs Blog, Feb 9, 2012, "Testing Projections for 2011") directly compared Marcel, PECOTA, Oliver, ZiPS, CAIRO, and Steamer on 2011 actual results, normalizing all systems to league-average wOBA (.330) and ERA (3.82). For hitters with 200+ PA: PECOTA RMSE .0317/AAE .0248/corr .6136; ZiPS RMSE .0318/AAE .0244/corr .6139; Oliver RMSE .0321/AAE .0247/corr .6151; Steamer RMSE .0322/AAE .0247/corr .6039; Marcel RMSE .0330/AAE .0257/corr .5614; CAIRO RMSE .0333/AAE .0264/corr .5685. For pitchers with 40+ IP: Steamer RMSE .8324/AAE .7067/corr .4581 (best on all three metrics, attributed to velocity data integration); CAIRO RMSE .8736/AAE .7331/corr .4213; SIERA_proj RMSE .8746/AAE .7281/corr .4089. — [Testing Projections for 2011, FanGraphs Blog](https://blogs.fangraphs.com/testing-projections-for-2011/) (non-peer-reviewed, 2012)
- Dan Meyer (The Hardball Times, Dec 18, 2014, "Evaluating The 2014 Projection Systems") compared Marcel, ZiPS, Oliver, PECOTA, and Steamer on wOBA accuracy across 177,967 plate appearances of hitter data. Overall MAE: ZiPS .0274 (best), Steamer .0277, PECOTA .0279, Oliver .0280, Marcel .0289 (worst). R² of projected-vs-actual: ZiPS .330 (best), Steamer .316, PECOTA .304, Oliver .307, Marcel .240 (worst). ZiPS was reported as particularly strong on rookies, veterans, and extreme breakout/breakdown seasons. — [Evaluating The 2014 Projection Systems, The Hardball Times](https://tht.fangraphs.com/evaluating-the-2014-projection-systems/) (non-peer-reviewed, 2014)
- Beyond the Box Score's "2015 projection systems in review" two-part series (Jan 14 and Jan 21, 2016) compared Steamer, ZiPS, PECOTA, and Marcel for both hitters and pitchers using 2015 actual outcomes; reported that for pitchers, Marcel's projections beat at least one of PECOTA or ZiPS in every category tested, attributed in part to Marcel's much lower standard deviation of error (.527) vs. PECOTA (.652) and ZiPS (.721), even though long-run (2006-2011) overall pitcher RMSE ranked SIERA 1.126 (best), Marcel 1.132, PECOTA 1.141, ZiPS 1.143 (worst). — [Part one: hitters](https://www.beyondtheboxscore.com/2016/1/14/10733872/steamer-zips-pecota-marcel-projections-review); [Part two: pitchers](https://www.beyondtheboxscore.com/2016/1/21/10795210/marcel-pecota-steamer-zips-pitchers-projections-review) (non-peer-reviewed, 2016) — note: these RMSE/std-dev figures for the 2006-2011 period could not be independently re-verified beyond the search-result summary; treat as secondhand from the article.
- Geoff Wranko (RotoValue) conducted an independent 2013-season test of wOBA accuracy across systems, discussed on tangotiger.com: Steamer ranked first (RMSE .0330), Consensus second (.0345), CAIRO third (.0347), Marcel as baseline (.0368), and Wranko's own RotoValue system placed last (.0356) — Wranko published this despite his own system underperforming. — [Who's Testing the Forecasting Systems?, tangotiger.com](https://tangotiger.com/index.php/site/comments/whos-testing-the-forecasting-systems) (non-peer-reviewed blog discussion, Jan 31, 2014)
- Colin Wyers (Baseball Prospectus, Feb 7, 2011) built a simplified version of PECOTA and ran it head-to-head against Marcel across 1950-2010 using RMSE and a "success rate" (percentage of head-to-head player comparisons won). Result: offense was "a dead heat" on RMSE with PECOTA slightly ahead on success rate; pitching showed identical RMSE with Marcel marginally ahead. Wyers explicitly notes this test favored breadth over depth and stripped PECOTA of real advantages (park adjustments, minor-league data), so it understates PECOTA's real-world edge. — [Reintroducing PECOTA: They're Here!, Baseball Prospectus](https://www.baseballprospectus.com/news/article/12884/reintroducing-pecota-theyre-here/) (non-peer-reviewed, 2011)
- FantasyPros' annual "Most Accurate Fantasy Baseball Projections" studies (methodology: z-score standardized errors across standard 5x5 roto categories — R, HR, RBI, SB, AVG for hitters; W, SV, K, ERA, WHIP for pitchers, summed across categories, lower = better) found: 2024 results — THE BAT X ranked #1 overall (score -1.874), ahead of FantasyPros Zeile Consensus (#2, -1.867) and FanGraphs ATC (#3, -1.857); Steamer ranked #6 (-0.649); ZiPS DC ranked #8 (-0.138). This was reported as the first time an "original" (non-consensus) system beat all consensus/aggregate systems. For 2023, THE BAT X ranked #3 overall, with FantasyPros Zeile Consensus #4. — [Most Accurate Fantasy Baseball Projections (2024 Results), FantasyPros, Feb 4 2025](https://www.fantasypros.com/2025/02/most-accurate-fantasy-baseball-projections-2024-results/); [2023 Results](https://www.fantasypros.com/2024/01/most-accurate-fantasy-baseball-projections-2023/) (non-peer-reviewed, industry fantasy-sports methodology, not scientific/academic validation)
- A FanGraphs RotoGraphs piece by Mike Podhorzer (Oct 14, 2024) did a qualitative head-to-head on wOBA forecasts between THE BAT X and Steamer for 2024, but did NOT report formal RMSE/MAE/correlation — only counted which system's forecast was closer for a hand-picked set of divergent players (THE BAT X correct on 4 of 6 "THE BAT X favorite" players; Steamer correct on 7 of 19 "Steamer favorite" players). This is an illustrative, not rigorous, comparison. — [2024 Projection Showdown — THE BAT X vs Steamer Hitter wOBA Forecasts — A Review, FanGraphs](https://fantasy.fangraphs.com/2024-projection-showdown-the-bat-x-vs-steamer-hitter-woba-forecasts-a-review/) (non-peer-reviewed, 2024)
- Gerber, E. & Craig, B., "A mixed effects multinomial logistic-normal model for forecasting baseball performance," Journal of Quantitative Analysis in Sports, Vol. 17, Issue 3, pp. 221-239, published online Jan 6, 2021 (DOI 10.1515/jqas-2020-0007) — this is a genuine peer-reviewed JQAS paper that models seasonal plate-appearance outcome vectors jointly via a mixed-effects multinomial logistic-normal (MLN) model, explicitly benchmarking its predictions against "Marcels, the most established publicly accessible prediction methodology" and against a standard (non-mixed) multinomial logistic model. The model produces a joint posterior predictive distribution for outcome vectors (capturing correlations between outcome types within and across player-seasons) rather than a single point estimate. — [DOI page](https://doi.org/10.1515/jqas-2020-0007); [De Gruyter Brill page](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/jqas-2020-0007/html) (peer-reviewed, JQAS, 2021)

### Inferences
- No single accuracy comparison is "the" ranking — results flip depending on year, player-performance stat (wOBA vs. ERA), sample filter (PA/IP threshold), and whether the test is single-year or multi-year/long-run. ZiPS and Steamer most often edge out Marcel and PECOTA in single-season hitter wOBA tests (2011, 2014 data), while Marcel's conservativeness (lower variance of error) sometimes gives it better long-run RMSE for pitchers despite worse single-year correlation.
- THE BAT X has the strongest recent (2023-2024) rigorous, repeated-methodology accuracy track record among "original" systems in the FantasyPros z-score framework, but that framework targets fantasy-relevant 5x5 roto categories, not sabermetric rate stats (wOBA/FIP) or academically validated error metrics.
- The field lacks a true apples-to-apples, peer-reviewed benchmark across all five named systems (Marcel, PECOTA, ZiPS, Steamer, THE BAT) on wOBA/OBP/SLG/ERA/FIP with RMSE/MAE/correlation; essentially all rigorous comparisons that exist are sabermetric blog/magazine pieces, most over a decade old (2011-2016), predating THE BAT/THE BAT X's prominence.

### Gaps
- No comparison found that includes THE BAT (non-X) or THE BAT X head-to-head against Marcel/PECOTA/ZiPS/Steamer using RMSE/MAE/correlation on wOBA, OBP, SLG, ERA, or FIP specifically (as opposed to fantasy roto-category z-scores).
- Could not access the full text of Gerber & Craig (2021) directly (ResearchGate PDF blocked with 403); only the abstract/summary was retrievable, so the specific quantitative results of the MLN-vs-Marcels comparison (e.g., log-loss or RMSE values) are unconfirmed — flagged as a gap rather than inferred.
- No JQAS or other peer-reviewed paper found that directly benchmarks PECOTA, ZiPS, or Steamer against each other (only Marcel appears as a benchmark in the one academic paper located).
- Could not verify the Beyond the Box Score 2006-2011 long-run pitcher RMSE/std-dev figures beyond the search-engine-generated summary; the original articles returned connection errors (ECONNRESET) on both direct fetch attempts, so these numbers rest on a secondhand synthesis.

## What is the core methodology of each system?

### Takeaway
All five systems share a common skeleton (multi-year weighted historical performance + regression to the mean + aging adjustment) but differ sharply in sophistication: Marcel is a deliberately minimal three-number recipe; PECOTA layers on a nearest-neighbor/comparable-player search; ZiPS and Steamer use more granular statistical regression (ZiPS adds DIPS-theory-based pitching analysis); THE BAT/THE BAT X layer in pitch-level Statcast data ("stuff" modeling) on top of a base projection.

### Cited Findings
- **Marcel ("Marcel the Monkey Forecasting System"), creator Tom Tango**: Uses 3 years of MLB data with the most recent season weighted most heavily (the commonly cited weighting is 5/4/3 for the most recent three years), then regresses each stat toward the league mean based on sample size, then applies a simple age adjustment. For players without an MLB track record, playing time defaults to "the league mean over 200 PA, 60 IP (starter) or 25 IP (reliever)." Explicitly described by Tango as "the most basic forecasting system you can have, that uses as little intelligence as possible" — a deliberate baseline, not meant to be the best system, "not persuaded by player comparison," ignores injury context, and does not use minor-league data. — [Marcel 2012, tangotiger.net](https://tangotiger.net/marcel/) (methodology primary source, Tom Tango)
- **PECOTA (Player Empirical Comparison and Optimization Test Algorithm), Baseball Prospectus, originally developed by Nate Silver**: Core method is identifying historical "comparable" players (nearest-neighbor/comp approach) and blending their career performance/aging trajectories into forward projections; uses major-league data from 1950-2010 plus (where available) minor-league data, with park-neutral baselines and age-adjusted forecasts. Baseball Prospectus explicitly notes that real PECOTA projections use "a lot more players" than the handful of comps publicly displayed per player. — [Reintroducing PECOTA: They're Here!, Baseball Prospectus, Colin Wyers, Feb 7 2011](https://www.baseballprospectus.com/news/article/12884/reintroducing-pecota-theyre-here/)
- **ZiPS (sZymborski Projection System), creator Dan Szymborski**: Uses weighted averages across 4 years of data (narrowed to 3 years for very young or very old players); for pitchers, regresses using DIPS (Defense-Independent Pitching Statistics) theory and BABIP rates; adjusts aging curves by analyzing trends among comparable historical players. — [The Projection Rundown: The Basics on Marcels, ZiPS, CAIRO, Oliver, and the Rest, FanGraphs Library](https://library.fangraphs.com/the-projection-rundown-the-basics-on-marcels-zips-cairo-oliver-and-the-rest/) (non-peer-reviewed explainer)
- **Steamer, creators Jared Cross (and collaborators)**: A regression-based system; Jared Cross is described as building "mathematical models to project the performance of professional baseball players," with the full algorithm rerun nightly. Known components include regression/shrinkage calibrated per population (e.g., different regression strength for low-minors pitchers), aging-curve adjustments, and league-level offensive-environment adjustments (particularly relevant for minor leaguers and players coming from foreign leagues). The 2011 FanGraphs test attributed Steamer's strong pitcher performance specifically to its incorporation of pitch-velocity data. — [Steamer Projections Updated, FanGraphs](https://blogs.fangraphs.com/instagraphs/steamer-projections-updated/); [Testing Projections for 2011, FanGraphs Blog](https://blogs.fangraphs.com/testing-projections-for-2011/) (non-peer-reviewed)
- **THE BAT / THE BAT X, creator Derek Carty**: THE BAT X differentiates itself from the base THE BAT system by incorporating Statcast data. For pitchers, it layers on a "stuff" model called THE BATcast using Statcast pitch-level data (velocity, movement, spin, etc.). For hitters, since 2020 it incorporates Statcast batted-ball data: barrels, exit velocity, launch angle, sprint speed, and derived variations found to be predictive. Carty describes testing to find "the optimal combination of THE BAT and THE BATcast" to form the final THE BAT X blended projection, and frames it as theoretically and empirically (via back-tests) the best of the three (THE BAT, THE BATcast, THE BAT X) because it starts from THE BAT's base and layers on the new Statcast information. — [Introducing THE BAT X, FanGraphs](https://fantasy.fangraphs.com/introducing-the-bat-x/); [THE BAT X, RotoGrinders](https://rotogrinders.com/the-bat) (non-peer-reviewed)
- **Oliver (Brian Cartwright) and CAIRO (Chris Cwik/collaborators), for context**: Oliver takes a 3-year weighted average with aging/regression adjustments but uses a distinct major-league-equivalency (MLE) calculation based on park/league adjustments rather than minor-league level transitions, and includes fielding/WAR components. CAIRO explicitly starts from the Marcel framework and layers on minor-league stats, park/league effects, position-specific aging curves, and uses 50,000 Diamond Mind simulations for team-level projections. — [The Projection Rundown, FanGraphs Library](https://library.fangraphs.com/the-projection-rundown-the-basics-on-marcels-zips-cairo-oliver-and-the-rest/) (non-peer-reviewed)

### Inferences
- There is a clear sophistication gradient: Marcel (minimal, 3 stats-only recipe) → Oliver/CAIRO (Marcel + minor-league/park adjustments) → ZiPS/Steamer (full regression models with DIPS theory and velocity/environment inputs) → PECOTA (comparable-player blending with large historical comp pools) → THE BAT X (regression base + Statcast-derived "stuff"/quality-of-contact layer). THE BAT X is the only system among those researched that formally incorporates pitch- and batted-ball-level Statcast data as a documented, named methodological layer.
- Despite large methodological differences, the three-ingredient recipe (multi-year weighted history + regression to the mean + aging curve) is the common denominator across every system studied, including the most advanced ones — Statcast/comp-based layers appear to function as refinements on top of this base rather than replacements for it.

### Gaps
- No primary-source methodology documentation (e.g., an official Baseball Prospectus technical paper, a ZiPS technical writeup by Szymborski, or a Steamer technical paper by Cross) was directly retrieved — all methodology descriptions here come from secondary explainer sources (FanGraphs Library, Baseball Prospectus narrative article) rather than the systems' own formal technical documentation, which may not be publicly published in full detail for any of these proprietary systems.
- THE BAT (non-X) base methodology (pre-Statcast) was not described in detail beyond "the base THE BAT system" — specific inputs/regression approach for the original THE BAT were not found in this research pass.

## What accuracy numbers have been reported, and under what validation methodology?

### Takeaway
Reported accuracy numbers cluster around three independent study designs: (1) single-season RMSE/AAE/correlation tests against realized outcomes (2011, 2014, 2015 data; FanGraphs/THT/Beyond the Box Score), (2) a 60-year (1950-2010) RMSE and win-rate test by Baseball Prospectus comparing a simplified PECOTA to Marcel, and (3) modern (2023-2025) z-score-based fantasy-category accuracy rankings by FantasyPros. All are summarized with exact figures in the first key question above; no additional validation methodologies beyond what's cited there were found.

### Cited Findings
- See the detailed numeric findings already cited under Key Question 1 (Swartz 2011 test; Meyer 2014 test; Beyond the Box Score 2015/2016 series; Wranko/tangotiger 2013 test; Wyers/BP 1950-2010 test; FantasyPros 2023/2024 z-score rankings) — all sources reproduced there to avoid duplication.
- Validation population/filters varied materially across studies: Swartz (2012) used 200+ PA hitters and 40+ IP pitchers; Meyer (2014) used all hitters excluding pitchers across 177,967 total PA; Wyers/BP (2011) used all MLB player-seasons 1950-2010; FantasyPros (2023-2025) used rostered/fantasy-relevant players scored on 5x5 roto categories rather than raw rate stats. — [Testing Projections for 2011](https://blogs.fangraphs.com/testing-projections-for-2011/); [Evaluating The 2014 Projection Systems](https://tht.fangraphs.com/evaluating-the-2014-projection-systems/); [Reintroducing PECOTA](https://www.baseballprospectus.com/news/article/12884/reintroducing-pecota-theyre-here/); [FantasyPros 2024 Results](https://www.fantasypros.com/2025/02/most-accurate-fantasy-baseball-projections-2024-results/)

### Inferences
- Because validation population and stat choice differ so much study to study, rankings are not directly comparable across years — e.g., Marcel's relatively strong long-run pitcher RMSE (per Beyond the Box Score) coexists with its weakest-of-the-group performance in the single-year 2011 and 2014 hitter tests; this is consistent with Marcel's low-variance, "regress hard, don't get fancy" design producing smaller worst-case errors at the cost of weaker correlation/sharpness in any given year.

### Gaps
- No large-sample, multi-year, pre-registered or peer-reviewed accuracy validation was found; all multi-year studies (Wyers/BP 1950-2010, Beyond the Box Score 2006-2011) are magazine/blog analyses, not academically peer-reviewed with disclosed full methodology/code.
- No validation numbers found for THE BAT (non-X) or THE BAT X on classic rate stats (wOBA/OBP/SLG/ERA/FIP) with RMSE/MAE/correlation — only fantasy 5x5-category z-score rankings (FantasyPros) and one qualitative, non-statistical FanGraphs comparison (Podhorzer 2024) were found for THE BAT X.

## Has Tom Tango or other analysts published formal accuracy comparisons of Marcel vs. other systems?

### Takeaway
Yes — Tom Tango has not run exhaustive comparisons himself but has actively curated/hosted community-submitted comparisons on tangotiger.com and publicly advocated that all forecasting systems should be required to publish after-the-fact accuracy results, treating any system that doesn't as "Marcel-equivalent until proven superior." The most substantive comparisons were produced by other analysts (Matt Swartz, Dan Meyer, Colin Wyers, Geoff Wranko) rather than by Tango himself.

### Cited Findings
- In a tangotiger.com post titled "Who's Testing the Forecasting Systems?" (Jan 31, 2014), Tango argued: "It should actually be a requirement that anyone that does forecasting that they publish after-the-fact results," and took the position that forecasters failing to publish results should be presumed to perform at Marcel-level (i.e., Marcel as the default baseline/null hypothesis for comparison). The same post surfaces Geoff Wranko's (RotoValue) 2013 wOBA RMSE comparison: Steamer .0330 (1st), Consensus .0345 (2nd), CAIRO .0347 (3rd), Marcel .0368 (baseline), RotoValue .0356 (actually better than Marcel's .0368, despite the search summary's listed rank order placing RotoValue last — note the apparent inconsistency between "RotoValue placed last" and RotoValue's RMSE of .0356 being numerically better than Marcel's .0368; this discrepancy was not resolved in the retrieved content and should be treated as an open inconsistency in the source, possibly reflecting a different set of systems in the full ranked list not fully captured in the fetched summary). — [Who's Testing the Forecasting Systems?, tangotiger.com](https://tangotiger.com/index.php/site/comments/whos-testing-the-forecasting-systems) (non-peer-reviewed, 2014)
- Tango's own Marcel release notes (tangotiger.net) assert, without detailed statistics, that Marcel's accuracy is comparable to "the best in the business" with only minimal performance differences versus more sophisticated systems — a claim made by Tango himself but not accompanied by a rigorous side-by-side statistical writeup on that page. — [Marcel 2012, tangotiger.net](https://tangotiger.net/marcel/) (primary source, non-peer-reviewed)
- Colin Wyers' Baseball Prospectus comparison (2011) is the most rigorous non-Tango comparison located, using RMSE and head-to-head win-rate over 60 years of data to show PECOTA and Marcel roughly at parity. — [Reintroducing PECOTA, Baseball Prospectus](https://www.baseballprospectus.com/news/article/12884/reintroducing-pecota-theyre-here/) (2011)

### Inferences
- Tango's own stated role is more as a methodology gatekeeper/convener (demanding transparency and treating Marcel as the baseline standard) than as the author of the most detailed quantitative comparisons; the heavy-lifting empirical comparisons were done by Swartz, Meyer, Wyers, and Wranko, with Tango's blog serving as a hub where these results were discussed and critiqued.

### Gaps
- Could not resolve the apparent numeric inconsistency in the Wranko RMSE rankings noted above (RotoValue's .0356 vs. Marcel's .0368, yet RotoValue described as "placing last") — flagged rather than silently resolved; a direct re-read of the original tangotiger.com post (beyond the fetched summary) would be needed to clarify whether additional unlisted systems filled the ranks between Marcel and RotoValue.
- No separate, later (post-2014) Tango-authored or Tango-hosted formal accuracy comparison was found; the most recent rigorous comparisons identified (FantasyPros 2023-2025) are run by a different organization using fantasy-specific scoring, not classic rate-stat RMSE/correlation of the kind Tango's community favored.

## General note on peer-review coverage

Only one genuinely peer-reviewed source was located that engages with any of these five named systems: Gerber & Craig (2021), JQAS 17(3), which benchmarks a new mixed-effects multinomial logistic-normal model against Marcels. No peer-reviewed source was found that benchmarks PECOTA, ZiPS, Steamer, or THE BAT/THE BAT X against each other or against Marcel using RMSE/MAE/correlation on wOBA/OBP/SLG/ERA/FIP. All quantitative head-to-head accuracy figures compiled in this report come from sabermetric blogs/magazines (FanGraphs, The Hardball Times, Beyond the Box Score, Baseball Prospectus, tangotiger.com) or from the fantasy-industry outlet FantasyPros, and should be clearly labeled as non-peer-reviewed when used in any downstream report.
