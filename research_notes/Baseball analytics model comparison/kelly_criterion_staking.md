# Kelly Criterion and Its Variants for Sports Betting Bankroll Management

## What is the classical Kelly criterion and how is it applied to sports betting staking?

### Takeaway
The Kelly criterion (Kelly 1956, building on earlier information-theoretic work; popularized for gambling/investing by Ed Thorp from the 1960s onward) gives a closed-form optimal bet fraction, f* = (bp − q)/b, that maximizes the long-run expected logarithmic growth rate of bankroll; this is general gambling/finance theory, not sports-specific or baseball-specific, but is the universal starting point cited in every sports-betting staking discussion.

### Cited Findings
- The standard sports-betting form of the Kelly formula is Kelly % = (b×p − q) / b, where b = decimal odds minus 1 (net odds received), p = bettor's estimated win probability, and q = 1 − p — [topendsports.com Kelly Criterion Calculator](https://www.topendsports.com/sports-betting/tools/kelly-criterion-calculator/)
- The optimal fraction of current capital to wager to maximize the growth coefficient is f* = (bp − q)/b, derived from maximizing expected log wealth; capital after n trials under fixed-fraction betting is X_n = X_0(1+f)^S(1−f)^F where S, F are wins/losses — [Wikipedia: Kelly criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_criterion); [Thorp, "The Kelly Criterion in Blackjack, Sports Betting, and the Stock Market" (2006)](https://gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/2006-thorp.pdf)
- Thorp built the Kelly approach for blackjack in 1962 (originally derived from Shannon-era information/signal theory by J.L. Kelly in 1956), then later applied the same log-utility framework professionally to hedge fund management — [Thorp (2006) via gwern.net](https://gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/2006-thorp.pdf)
- Only the logarithmic utility function maximizes asymptotic long-run wealth and minimizes the expected time to reach arbitrarily large wealth goals — a mathematical theorem compiled across the definitive edited volume by MacLean, Thorp & Ziemba — [MacLean, Thorp & Ziemba (eds.), *The Kelly Capital Growth Investment Criterion: Theory and Practice* (World Scientific, 2011), SSRN overview](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1797366); [Tandfonline review of the volume](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14697688.2011.619561)
- In an MLB-style example: a team priced at +150 (~40% implied probability) but believed to have a true 45% win probability has a 5-point edge that the Kelly formula converts into a specific bet-size recommendation — [Circa Sports, "Kelly Criterion for Sports Betting Explained"](https://www.circasports.com/blog/kelly-criterion-for-sports-betting-explained)

### Inferences
- The formula itself is sport-agnostic; its correctness for any single sports bet depends entirely on p being accurate, which is the opening for all the "fractional"/"uncertainty-aware" extensions covered below.

### Gaps
- No MLB-specific primary academic source computing or validating the classical (unmodified) Kelly formula directly on MLB moneyline/run-line data was found; all sports-betting framing sources above are general/practitioner treatments rather than peer-reviewed baseball studies.

## What does the fractional Kelly literature say about variance reduction, uncertainty, and the growth-rate tradeoff?

### Takeaway
Fractional Kelly (betting a fixed multiple f of the full-Kelly stake, e.g., f=0.5 "half-Kelly" or f=0.25 "quarter-Kelly") is standard practitioner and academic-adjacent practice (MacLean/Thorp/Ziemba capital-growth theory) because it trades a predictable, quantifiable fraction of growth rate for a much larger reduction in variance/drawdown risk, and it is treated in the literature as a blended strategy between the Kelly-optimal risky allocation and holding cash.

### Cited Findings
- Half-Kelly captures roughly 75% of the geometric growth rate of full Kelly while incurring only about half the variance; quarter-Kelly captures roughly 44% of full-Kelly growth with one-quarter of the variance — [topendsports.com Kelly Criterion Calculator](https://www.topendsports.com/sports-betting/tools/kelly-criterion-calculator/) (practitioner source, consistent with the general quadratic approximation of the Kelly growth-vs-variance tradeoff described in the academic literature below)
- A fractional Kelly bet is computed by scaling the Kelly-optimal bet by f ∈ (0,1); this scales down risky exposure by f, producing smaller drawdowns than full Kelly "at the cost of reduced growth rate" — [Risk-Constrained Kelly Gambling, Busseti, Ryu & Boyd](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/pdf/kelly.pdf); summarized also in [Frontiers in Applied Mathematics and Statistics (2020)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/applied-mathematics-and-statistics/articles/10.3389/fams.2020.577050/full)
- Betting at 30% of the Kelly-optimal amount reduces the probability of an 80% drawdown from roughly 1-in-5 (full Kelly) to roughly 1-in-213, while retaining about 51% of the full-Kelly growth rate — a specific quantified example of the fractional-Kelly risk/growth tradeoff — [search-aggregated from Kelly-criterion technical sources citing MacLean/Ziemba-style drawdown formulas](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.01787) ("On Kelly Betting: Some Limitations")
- The MacLean/Thorp/Ziemba volume explicitly frames fractional Kelly as a strategy that "blends the Kelly suggested wager with cash," reducing the short-term risk of considerable loss at the cost of lower expected final wealth — [MacLean, Thorp & Ziemba (eds.), *The Kelly Capital Growth Investment Criterion*, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1797366)
- Thorp's own practical guidance is to multiply the full-Kelly stake by 0.25–0.5 depending on confidence in the probability estimate, because "your estimate of p is always softer than the formula assumes" — [Thorp (2006), via gwern.net](https://gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/2006-thorp.pdf)
- In a controlled experimental review across horse racing, basketball (NBA), and football data, when the bettor's predictive model was actually worse than the bookmaker's (negative KL edge), both full Kelly and a Maximum-Sharpe strategy produced a 100% ruin rate, whereas fractional Kelly avoided this outcome and was judged "the most suitable option out of the strategies reviewed" because it achieved "the best, or close to the best, performance... while being comparatively simpler" — [Optimal sports betting strategies in practice: an experimental review, arXiv:2107.08827](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2107.08827)

### Inferences
- The variance-reduction rationale for fractional Kelly is mathematically distinct from (but often conflated in practitioner writing with) the parameter-uncertainty rationale: fixed fractional Kelly reduces variance even if p is known exactly, whereas the uncertainty-driven shrinkage methods below (Baker & McHale; Chu, Wu & Swartz) derive a fraction specifically as a function of how uncertain p is.
- The experimental review's finding that full Kelly can produce 100% ruin when the model is worse than the market is a strong empirical caution relevant to MLB bettors who may overestimate their edge versus efficient MLB markets.

### Gaps
- No MLB/baseball-specific empirical test of the half-Kelly/quarter-Kelly growth-vs-variance tradeoff numbers (75%/44% growth retention figures) was found; those figures come from general Kelly theory and practitioner betting-calculator sources, not a baseball dataset.

## What does the literature say about Kelly staking under model/probability uncertainty (Bayesian/robust/shrinkage Kelly)?

### Takeaway
A specific, directly sports-betting-relevant academic literature exists on this exact problem: Baker & McHale (2013) derive a formal shrinkage factor that discounts the naive Kelly stake to account for sampling/estimation uncertainty in the win probability, tested on tennis data; Chu, Wu & Swartz (2018, *Journal of Quantitative Analysis in Sports*) build a decision-theoretic "modified Kelly" that similarly produces a smaller-than-naive fraction, tested on horse racing, basketball, and soccer; and separate robust-optimization work applies a min-max treatment of probability uncertainty to horse-race betting.

### Cited Findings
- Baker & McHale show that because the Kelly criterion normally assumes the "true" win probability is known with certainty, naively replacing it with a sample/model estimate gives "poorer out-of-sample than in-sample performance"; their correction is to shrink the bet size in the presence of parameter uncertainty — [Baker & McHale, "Optimal Betting Under Parameter Uncertainty: Improving the Kelly Criterion" (search-aggregated abstract via ResearchGate/Semantic Scholar)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/262425087_Optimal_Betting_Under_Parameter_Uncertainty_Improving_the_Kelly_Criterion)
- Baker & McHale provide an "approximate... back of envelope" shrinkage-factor correction usable directly by bettors, and the optimal shrinkage factor depends on the estimated uncertainty (variance) of the winning-probability estimate; they note that for the log-utility (standard Kelly) case the correction is always a shrinkage (smaller bet), whereas for other utility functions the optimal bet can in rare, very-favorable-odds cases actually swell rather than shrink — [Baker & McHale, Decision Analytics (2013), as summarized via search aggregation of the paper](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Optimal-Betting-Under-Parameter-Uncertainty:-the-Baker-McHale/d03cda6e9aec9a6674047b1c093780fdc2bf2d56)
- Baker & McHale validate the shrunken-Kelly approach via both a simulation study and an analysis of real tennis betting data, finding the shrunken-Kelly approaches "offer an improvement over the raw Kelly criterion" — [Baker & McHale paper summary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/262425087_Optimal_Betting_Under_Parameter_Uncertainty_Improving_the_Kelly_Criterion) (tennis-specific, NOT baseball — labeled general sports-betting literature)
- Chu, Wu & Swartz, "Modified Kelly Criteria," explicitly motivate their work by noting that "the probability of placing a correct wager is unknown" in real sports wagering, and derive modified Kelly criteria using a decision-theoretic framework rather than ad-hoc fractions like half-Kelly or quarter-Kelly, which they note "have no theoretical underpinnings"; their resultant modified Kelly fractions are systematically smaller than the naive Kelly fraction — [Chu, Wu & Swartz, *Journal of Quantitative Analysis in Sports*, 14(1), 2018, pp. 1-11](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/jqas-2017-0122/html?lang=en); [preprint PDF](https://www.sfu.ca/~tswartz/papers/kelly.pdf)
- Chu, Wu & Swartz test their modified Kelly scheme against the original Kelly criterion via simulation across three sports — horse racing, basketball, and soccer — over betting "seasons" of length 100 through 5000 bets, reporting performance under a unified evaluation protocol — [same source, JQAS 14(1), 2018](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/jqas-2017-0122/html?lang=en)
- A separate robust-optimization paper on horse-race betting explicitly models uncertainty in probability estimates by defining "uncertainty sets"/ambiguity regions around the point estimate and solving a worst-case (min-max) optimization over that set rather than using a single point estimate, reporting reduced portfolio volatility and more conservative, more robust betting patterns versus standard point-estimate Kelly — [Kelly betting on horse races with uncertainty in probability estimates, arXiv:1701.02814](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.02814)
- The experimental-review paper separately tested a "distributionally robust" Kelly approach (citing Sun & Boyd 2018) across horse racing/basketball/football and found it was "the safest in all of the experiments," with the highest minimum final wealth across simulated scenarios, but at the cost of lower median profit (e.g., 6.2x vs. 10.25x final wealth in the football dataset, versus a drawdown-constrained fractional approach) — [Optimal sports betting strategies in practice, arXiv:2107.08827](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2107.08827)

### Inferences
- There is a clear, direct lineage of peer-reviewed work (Baker & McHale 2013; Chu/Wu/Swartz 2018; the robust/distributionally-robust horse-racing line) that treats "discount the Kelly stake because p is estimated, not known" as a formal statistical problem with a derivable, principled shrinkage factor — this is materially stronger theoretical grounding than simply picking an arbitrary fraction like 0.25 or 0.5.
- None of this uncertainty-aware literature is baseball/MLB-specific; the empirical validations are tennis (Baker & McHale), horse racing/basketball/soccer (Chu, Wu & Swartz), and horse racing (the arXiv robust paper). This should be explicitly flagged as cross-sport generalization when applied to MLB.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the exact closed-form shrinkage-factor equation from Baker & McHale (ResearchGate/Semantic Scholar blocked full-text extraction; only the qualitative description above was retrievable) — the report-writer should treat the shrinkage-factor functional form as "depends on estimated variance of the win-probability estimate" without a verified exact formula.
- No paper applying either the Baker-McHale shrinkage approach or the Chu/Wu/Swartz modified-Kelly framework specifically to MLB/baseball betting markets was found.

## What literature covers dynamic Kelly strategies with drawdown constraints or stop-loss rules, and how do these differ architecturally from fixed fractional Kelly?

### Takeaway
A distinct strand of (mostly finance/control-theory, not sports-specific) academic literature formally embeds a drawdown or ruin-probability constraint directly into the Kelly optimization problem — producing a *dynamic* fraction that varies with the current drawdown level — which is mathematically and architecturally different from simply picking a fixed fractional-Kelly multiplier (e.g., quarter-Kelly) and bolting an independent stop-loss rule on top; sports-betting experimental work (Chu et al. 2107.08827) shows a drawdown-constrained Kelly variant achieving 0% ruin while retaining most of fractional Kelly's growth.

### Cited Findings
- Busseti, Ryu & Boyd ("Risk-Constrained Kelly Gambling") add an explicit constraint that "limits the probability of a drawdown of wealth to a given undesirable level" directly inside the gambling/portfolio optimization, rather than applying it as an external rule — [Busseti, Ryu & Boyd, "Risk-Constrained Kelly Gambling" (Stanford working paper / arXiv:1603.06183)](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/pdf/kelly.pdf); [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.06183)
- They derive a computable upper bound on drawdown probability that converts the (otherwise intractable) chance-constrained problem into a convex optimization problem, introducing a single risk-aversion parameter that lets the bettor/investor systematically trade off growth rate against drawdown-probability safety — [same source, arXiv:1603.06183](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.06183)
- Their risk-constrained bets are reported to "out-perform fractional-Kelly bets for the same drawdown risk level or growth rate" — i.e., for a matched level of drawdown protection, the risk-constrained solution grows wealth faster than simply scaling down to an equivalent fixed Kelly fraction — [same source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.06183)
- Davis & Lleo, "Fractional Kelly Strategies for Benchmarked Asset Management," extend fractional Kelly to the case where the investor's objective is to outperform a benchmark (rather than simply maximize absolute log-wealth), and show these benchmarked fractional-Kelly strategies remain efficient even when returns are not lognormally distributed; this is a finance-portfolio-theory extension, not sports-specific — [Davis & Lleo, in MacLean/Thorp/Ziemba (eds.), *The Kelly Capital Growth Investment Criterion*, World Scientific (2011); summary via ResearchGate/EconPapers](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228860076_Fractional_Kelly_Strategies_for_Benchmarked_Asset_Management)
- Separately, a drawdown-constrained-investment paper establishes that, under a formal drawdown constraint on long-term growth-optimal portfolios, the optimal strategy is a *dynamic* fractional Kelly strategy in which the fraction of wealth allocated to the Kelly-growth-optimal fund (versus a safe/baseline asset) depends on the *current* level of drawdown already experienced — i.e., the fraction itself is a state-dependent function of path history, not a fixed constant — [arXiv:1206.2305, "The numeraire property and long-term growth optimality for drawdown-constrained investments"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.2305) (full technical detail of the fraction's functional form could not be retrieved — see Gaps)
- In the sports-betting experimental review, a "drawdown-modified Kelly" strategy achieved 0% ruin across all three tested sports (horse racing, basketball, football) while still delivering strong wealth growth (10.25x median final wealth in the football dataset), outperforming the purely static fractional-Kelly comparison on the ruin-avoidance metric while remaining competitive on growth — [Optimal sports betting strategies in practice, arXiv:2107.08827](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2107.08827)

### Inferences
- Architecturally, these drawdown-aware approaches (Busseti/Ryu/Boyd; the dynamic-fractional-Kelly-under-drawdown-constraint line) differ from "quarter-Kelly plus an independent stop-loss" in a specific, important way: a fixed fractional Kelly with a bolted-on stop-loss is a two-stage heuristic (size bets at a constant fraction of full Kelly, then if a separately-defined loss threshold is breached, halt betting or cut stakes by a separate discretionary rule) — the stake fraction itself does not respond continuously to current drawdown, and the stop-loss boundary is not derived from the same growth-maximization objective. In contrast, the risk-constrained/dynamic approaches solve a single joint optimization in which the bet fraction is *continuously* and *endogenously* a function of current wealth/drawdown state, so the "stop-loss-like" behavior emerges from the same objective function that also sets ordinary bet sizing, and the tradeoff between growth and drawdown safety is governed by one tunable risk parameter instead of two independently-chosen heuristics (the Kelly fraction and the stop-loss threshold). This makes the dynamic approach theoretically cleaner but also considerably harder to implement than a fixed fraction with a simple stop-loss rule.
- The experimental review's drawdown-modified-Kelly result (0% ruin across sports, strong wealth growth) is the closest thing found to direct empirical sports-betting evidence that a principled drawdown-aware Kelly variant beats a naive fixed-fraction-only approach on ruin risk without materially sacrificing growth — but it is NOT baseball-specific (horse racing/basketball/football only).

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the exact mathematical form of the drawdown-dependent dynamic fraction from arXiv:1206.2305 (WebFetch only returned the abstract-level description); the report-writer should present the qualitative mechanism (fraction depends on current drawdown state) without claiming a specific formula.
- No literature was found applying any of these drawdown-constrained/dynamic-Kelly frameworks specifically to sports betting bankrolls in a peer-reviewed sports-analytics journal (the Chu et al. drawdown-modified Kelly in arXiv:2107.08827 is the closest, and it is itself a synthesis/comparison paper, not the originating theoretical contribution — the originating theory is from the Boyd/Busseti/Ryu and Davis/Lleo finance literature).

## Is there literature specifically on MLB/baseball Kelly betting, or on multi-bet/correlated-bet Kelly relevant to placing several simultaneous MLB bets on one day?

### Takeaway
No baseball/MLB-specific Kelly-criterion academic paper was found in this search; however, a distinct and directly relevant academic literature on simultaneous/correlated multi-bet Kelly optimization exists (Grant & Buchen 2013; general portfolio-Kelly theory), which is the correct frame for a bettor placing several MLB games' bets on the same day, since naively applying single-bet Kelly to each game independently overstates the safe total exposure when outcomes/edges are correlated or stacked.

### Cited Findings
- No paper specifically computing or testing a Kelly staking strategy on MLB baseball betting markets was found among search results; the closest baseball-adjacent academic authors identified (Tim Swartz and co-authors, who are established baseball/sports analytics researchers at Simon Fraser University's Sports Analytics Group) published their Kelly-uncertainty work (Chu, Wu & Swartz 2018) using horse racing, basketball, and soccer data — not baseball — [SFU Sports Analytics Group publications page](https://www.sfu.ca/sports-analytics-group/publications/); [Chu, Wu & Swartz, JQAS 14(1), 2018](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/jqas-2017-0122/html?lang=en)
- When multiple bets are placed simultaneously, the mathematically correct joint-optimal Kelly strategy requires optimizing over the full joint outcome space; a naive formulation scales as O(2^N) in time/memory for N simultaneous wagers, which is computationally intractable for more than a handful of games — [search-aggregated technical description, consistent with general Kelly-portfolio literature](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0803.1364) ("Diversification and limited information in the Kelly game")
- Grant & Buchen (2013), "A Comparison of Simultaneous Kelly Betting Strategies," build a Dirichlet-distribution-based simulation using empirical odds from the 2007-08 English Premier League season (soccer, NOT baseball) to compare Kelly strategies under simultaneous games, controlling for the size and noise of the bettor's edge — [Grant & Buchen, Journal of Gambling Business and Economics, 6(2), 2012/2013, pp. 1-28](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/bucjgbeco/v_3a6_3ay_3a2012_3ai_3a2_3ap_3a1-28.htm)
- Grant & Buchen's simulation results show that a Kelly strategy using "multibets of all levels" (i.e., using combination/parlay-style bets alongside single bets, jointly optimized) outperforms a portfolio-optimization approach that restricts betting to single-game outcomes only — [same source](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/bucjgbeco/v_3a6_3ay_3a2012_3ai_3a2_3ap_3a1-28.htm); corroborated in [Optimal sports betting strategies in practice, arXiv:2107.08827](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2107.08827), which separately notes Kelly multibet strategies outperform single-outcome portfolio optimization
- Practitioner-level treatment of correlated simultaneous bets (e.g., same-game markets or games with correlated outcomes) warns that computing full Kelly independently for each of several simultaneous bets and placing all of them results in systematic overbetting, since each bet's Kelly fraction is sized "as if it were the only exposure"; if bets are correlated, true joint risk is materially higher than the sum of independent-bet risk would suggest, and the practitioner recommendation is to cap total slate exposure and treat correlated bets as closer to a single position — [search-aggregated practitioner summary, consistent with academic portfolio-Kelly theory](https://joinparlayscience.com/blog/kelly-criterion-bet-sizing) (practitioner source, not peer-reviewed — flagged accordingly)
- Earlier foundational work on this exact problem is Grant, Johnstone & Buchen's original "Optimal Betting Strategies for Simultaneous Games" (published in *Decision Analysis*, INFORMS), which the Grant & Buchen (2013) simulation paper builds on — [Grant, Johnstone & Buchen, "Optimal Betting Strategies for Simultaneous Games," Decision Analysis (INFORMS)](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/deca.1080.0106)

### Inferences
- Because MLB bettors commonly place several same-day bets across the roughly 15 games on a typical MLB slate, the simultaneous/correlated-bet Kelly literature (Grant/Johnstone/Buchen; Grant & Buchen) is more directly actionable for this use case than the single-bet fractional-Kelly literature alone, even though none of it is baseball-specific — the core lesson (don't independently full-Kelly-size each of N simultaneous bets without accounting for joint/correlated risk; cap aggregate slate exposure) transfers directly to a same-day MLB betting slate, especially for correlated bets (e.g., two props tied to the same game, or MLB bets correlated via shared starting-pitcher or weather exposure).
- Most MLB games on a given slate are plausibly close to independent across different stadiums/pitchers (lower correlation than, say, same-game parlay legs), which would make the O(2^N) joint-optimization problem somewhat more tractable via independence-approximation shortcuts than in sports with more inherent same-slate correlation — but this is an inference, not a sourced finding, and no source directly addresses MLB's specific correlation structure.

### Gaps
- No MLB-specific paper or dataset applying simultaneous/correlated Kelly optimization to actual same-day MLB betting slates was found.
- Could not retrieve the specific numerical magnitude of the multibet-vs-single-bet performance advantage reported by Grant & Buchen (2013) beyond the qualitative "outperforms" finding — full-text access to the Journal of Gambling Business and Economics article was not available via the search/fetch tools used.
- No source was found directly quantifying typical outcome correlation across different MLB games played simultaneously on the same date (as opposed to correlation within a single game's multiple bet legs), which would be needed to size the practical correction factor for a same-day multi-game MLB betting slate.
