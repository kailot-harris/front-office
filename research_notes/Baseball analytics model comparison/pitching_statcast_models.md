# Pitching-Specific Statistical/ML Models in Baseball Analytics (Statcast-Based)

## Which papers/sources compare predictive power of ERA estimators (FIP, xFIP, SIERA, DRA) against each other and against Stuff+-style Statcast models?

### Takeaway
The most decisive, numbers-backed comparison found is Baseball Prospectus's "An Updated Evaluation of Hitting and Pitching (Including Stuff) Metrics," which ranks DRA > cFIP > xwOBA/xERA > xFIP ≈ SIERA > FIP on year-over-year predictive correlation, and separately shows that raw Stuff+ is predictive but confounded with team/park context (much weaker out-of-sample when a pitcher changes teams). SIERA's own introductory research (Baseball Prospectus, 2010) and subsequent secondary write-ups establish that SIERA and xFIP are both more predictive of next-year ERA than FIP or ERA itself, with SIERA holding a small edge, while FIP remains the best same-year descriptive fit.

### Cited Findings
- Baseball Prospectus's comparison of year-ahead (2021→2022) weighted Spearman "predictiveness" correlations: DRA (updated) 0.26, cFIP (updated) 0.25, xwOBA/xERA 0.23, xFIP 0.19, FIP 0.19, SIERA 0.18 — i.e., DRA and cFIP meaningfully outperform the FIP-family and Statcast-expected-stat metrics in this test. — [An Updated Evaluation of Hitting and Pitching (Including Stuff) Metrics](https://www.baseballprospectus.com/news/article/82426/prospectus-feature-an-updated-evaluation-of-hitting-and-pitching-including-stuff-metrics/)
- In the same BP analysis, raw pitch-level Stuff+ shows correlation to next-year run prevention of 0.41 for pitchers who stayed on the same team, but only 0.14 for pitchers who switched teams (0.33 across all pitchers combined) — strongly suggesting Stuff+ as typically computed captures team/park/catcher context as much as a portable pitcher skill. — [An Updated Evaluation of Hitting and Pitching (Including Stuff) Metrics](https://www.baseballprospectus.com/news/article/82426/prospectus-feature-an-updated-evaluation-of-hitting-and-pitching-including-stuff-metrics/)
- SIERA's introductory research found that when 2003–2008 coefficients were applied out-of-sample to predict 2009 park-adjusted ERA, SIERA "emerged as the best estimator," and the authors explicitly note it does not match FIP's same-season correlation because it treats home-run rate differently, trading descriptive fit for predictive fit. — [Introducing SIERA: Part 1 (Baseball Prospectus)](https://www.baseballprospectus.com/news/article/10027/introducing-siera-part-1/)
- A secondary analysis (not peer-reviewed, cites RMSE) reports SIERA's RMSE of 0.871 against following-year ERA as the lowest (best) among FIP, xFIP, and SIERA, with xFIP close behind; it characterizes xFIP and SIERA as "nearly interchangeable" descriptively and predictively, both well ahead of FIP as predictors of future performance even though FIP better explains in-season ERA. — [The Relative Value of FIP, xFIP, SIERA, and xERA Pt. II (PitcherList)](https://pitcherlist.com/the-relative-value-of-fip-xfip-siera-and-xera-pt-ii/)
- DRA's design rationale (Baseball Prospectus) is that it uses mixed (hierarchical/multilevel) regression models to isolate pitcher contribution from context — park, batter, catcher, umpire, defense — rather than relying on DIPS-style assumptions that pitchers have no control over batted-ball outcomes; it explicitly incorporates home runs and all balls in play, unlike FIP/xFIP, which is presented as the reason for its improved reliability and predictiveness. — [Introducing Deserved Run Average (DRA) And All Its Friends](https://www.baseballprospectus.com/news/article/26195/prospectus-feature-introducing-deserved-run-average-draand-all-its-friends/)
- FanGraphs' library page on Stuff+/Location+/Pitching+ reports pitch-level stabilization points — Stuff+ reliable at roughly 80 pitches, Location+ around 400 pitches, and Pitching+ outpredicting standard projection systems (lower RMSE vs. end-of-season ERA) after roughly 250 reliever pitches or 400+ starter pitches (~4-5 starts). — [Stuff+, Location+, and Pitching+ Primer (FanGraphs Library)](https://library.fangraphs.com/pitching/stuff-location-and-pitching-primer/)

### Inferences
- The strongest, most carefully validated single-number ERA estimators in this literature are not FIP-family stats but context-adjusted mixed-model stats (DRA, cFIP) — these outperform both FIP-family stats and raw pitch-characteristic Stuff+ models in the one head-to-head numeric comparison found.
- Raw "Stuff+"-style models trained on pitch physics alone appear to conflate team/environment effects with pitcher skill; a model builder adopting Stuff+ should be cautious about using it as a standalone predictive run-prevention metric without park/catcher/team controls or combining it with Location+/Pitching+ composites.
- SIERA and xFIP are both legitimately more predictive than raw FIP for forecasting future ERA, and both require only FanGraphs-leaderboard-level inputs (no pitch-tracking data), making them a much lower lift than Statcast-based models.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve a true peer-reviewed (JQAS or similar) head-to-head statistical comparison paper of FIP/xFIP/SIERA/DRA/Stuff+ with formal significance testing — the most rigorous comparison found (BP's "Updated Evaluation") is a sabermetric research article, not an academic journal publication, though it is methodologically careful (weighted Spearman correlations, reliability splits).
- No official academic (JQAS) citation was found specifically benchmarking Stuff+/Location+/Pitching+-style models against DRA in a formally published, peer-reviewed venue; this remains an industry/public-analytics (FanGraphs/Baseball Prospectus) literature rather than a university-journal literature.
- DRA's exact current-year quantitative reliability/predictiveness numbers from its most recent (2016+) formulation were not independently verified beyond the BP comparison table above.

## What specific published methodologies exist for pitch-quality modeling using Statcast data to predict pitcher run prevention?

### Takeaway
"Stuff+"-family models (Stuff+, Location+, Pitching+, and public variants like tjStuff+, aStuff+, PitchingBot) are supervised machine-learning models — typically tree-based (e.g., gradient-boosted/decision-tree regressors) — trained on pitch-level Statcast features (velocity, spin rate, spin axis/axis differential, release point, horizontal/vertical movement, location, count context, handedness) to predict a run-value outcome per pitch, then aggregated and index-scaled (100 = league average) to produce a pitcher-level or pitch-type-level quality score; this is a fundamentally different measurement approach than any FanGraphs-leaderboard ERA estimator because it requires full pitch-tracking (Statcast or PITCHf/x) ingestion, not box-score aggregates.

### Cited Findings
- Stuff+ is computed from per-pitch physical characteristics only (velocity, vertical/horizontal movement, release point, spin rate, and "axis differential" — the gap between movement predicted by spin alone and observed movement, attributable to seam-shifted wake) and is trained against observed run values using a decision-tree-based model to capture nonlinear interactions among these variables. — [Stuff+, Location+, and Pitching+ Primer (FanGraphs Library)](https://library.fangraphs.com/pitching/stuff-location-and-pitching-primer/)
- Location+ separately scores pitch placement quality given pitch type, count, and batter handedness context (location-only, independent of raw pitch physics), and Pitching+ combines physical characteristics, location, and count into one integrated run-value-based score. — [Stuff+, Location+, and Pitching+ Primer (FanGraphs Library)](https://library.fangraphs.com/pitching/stuff-location-and-pitching-primer/)
- The majority of public ML-based pitch-quality models are built either as a run-value regression model (predicting expected run value per pitch from its physical/location features) or as an outcome-classification model (e.g., predicting whiff probability); independent public implementations include tjStuff+ (Thomas Nestico), aStuff+ (Adam Salorio), and an open "pitchquality" expected-whiff model trained on 1.27 million Statcast pitches with held-out-season validation. — [GitHub: jakeyoung1/pitchquality](https://github.com/jakeyoung1/pitchquality); [Modelling tjStuff+ v1.0](https://medium.com/@thomasjamesnestico/modelling-tjstuff-d9a451765484); [Introducing aStuff+ v2](https://adamsalorio.substack.com/p/introducing-astuff-v2)
- Stuff+ models' inputs explicitly include release point, velocity, movement, spin rate, spin axis, and pitcher/batter handedness, with secondary (non-fastball) pitches additionally scored on velocity and movement differential relative to the pitcher's own primary fastball. — [Stuff+: The Future of Pitching Analytics](https://pitching.dev/stuff-the-future-of-pitching-analytics); [maxsportingstudio: Introducing Stuff+, Location+, and Pitching+](https://maxsportingstudio.com/introducing-stuff-location-and-pitching/)

### Inferences
- All Stuff+-family methods require full pitch-by-pitch Statcast ingestion (release point, spin rate/axis, movement, velocity, location per pitch) — this is categorically different data from FanGraphs/box-score leaderboard rate stats (K%, BB%, GB%) used by FIP/xFIP/SIERA, and represents a large new data-pipeline dependency for any system not already ingesting Statcast.
- Because these models are trained on run value (an outcome measure itself influenced by league/park/opponent context in the training data), they inherit some of the same context-confounding risk flagged in the DRA-vs-Stuff+ comparison above; public implementations vary in how carefully they control for this.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed/JQAS-published methodology paper for Stuff+ itself was found — the primary technical documentation is FanGraphs' public library/primer and independent analyst blogs/Substacks/GitHub repos (Nestico, Salorio, Young), not an academic journal. This is public/applied sabermetric research rather than formally peer-reviewed literature.
- Exact algorithm specifics (e.g., XGBoost vs. random forest vs. gradient boosting, hyperparameters, train/test split sizes) for FanGraphs' official Stuff+/Location+/Pitching+ were not confirmed beyond "decision tree-based model" language in the primer; full technical detail would require the original FanGraphs technical article(s), which were not directly fetched.

## Are there papers on pitch sequencing (pitch-type/location sequences and batter outcomes) with measurable predictive value?

### Takeaway
Yes — academic/conference-grade work exists at multiple levels of rigor: an MDPI Sports journal article links sequence-predictability/complexity to ERA and FIP; an MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference paper uses directed-graph embeddings over ~3.5 million pitches (2015-2019) to find quantitative evidence of "setup" and "knockout" pitch patterns; a newer (2026) arXiv paper uses a Transformer model and counterfactual simulation on Statcast data to estimate that optimizing pitch sequencing could shift season-level K/9 by more than 1.0; and a JQAS-track paper uses propensity-score stratified analysis to evaluate pitching strategy causally.

### Cited Findings
- "Pitch Sequence Complexity and Long-Term Pitcher Performance" (peer-reviewed, MDPI journal *Sports*, DOI 10.3390/sports3010040) built independent sequence models per pitcher's four most-frequent pitches using 2011–2013 pitch data, achieved 74.5% next-pitch-type predictability, and found that an index of pitch-sequence predictability/complexity projects pitcher performance such that reduced sequencing complexity correlates with higher (worse) ERA and FIP. — [Pitch Sequence Complexity and Long-Term Pitcher Performance](https://doi.org/10.3390/sports3010040) (full text at https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4663/3/1/40, access-blocked on fetch attempt — citation drawn from search-result synopsis)
- "Decoding MLB Pitch Sequencing Strategies via Directed Graph Embeddings" (15th MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference, 2021, Arnav Prasad) analyzed roughly 3.5 million pitches from 2015-2019 using graph embeddings plus Gaussian Mixture Model clustering (EM algorithm), finding that at-bats group into a finite set of universal pitch-type/zone-selection patterns, and producing quantitative evidence of "setup" and "knockout" pitches within sequence clusters; it also found pitchers dynamically alter sequencing in response to in-game matchup information rather than only following fixed season/career tendencies. — [Decoding MLB Pitch Sequencing Strategies via Directed Graph Embeddings](https://www.sloansportsconference.com/research-papers/decoding-mlb-pitch-sequencing-strategies-via-directed-graph-embeddings)
- "Counterfactual Optimization of Baseball Pitch Sequences and Estimation of Its Impact on Season-Level Statistics" (arXiv 2606.17345, Takamido & Nakamoto) trained a Transformer model on MLB Statcast data to predict whether a target pitch results in an in-play outcome vs. a swing-and-miss/take, then generated counterfactual sequences by substituting the final pitch or earlier "setup" pitches; the paper reports that jointly optimizing final and setup pitch choices could plausibly shift season-level performance by more than 1.0 in K/9. A revised version is titled "Machine learning modeling of hit-into-play probability and quantification of pitch sequence effects," with code released on GitHub. — [arXiv:2606.17345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17345)
- "Pitching strategy evaluation via stratified analysis using propensity score" (Journal of Quantitative Analysis in Sports, Vol. 19 Issue 2, 2023, DOI 10.1515/jqas-2021-0060) uses propensity-score stratification — a causal-inference technique — to evaluate pitching strategy effects, aiming to isolate the causal effect of a sequencing/strategy choice from confounding game-state factors. — [Pitching strategy evaluation via stratified analysis using propensity score](https://doi.org/10.1515/jqas-2021-0060) (methodology details not independently verified beyond title/abstract search; full-text fetch failed due to binary/corrupted PDF retrieval)

### Inferences
- Pitch sequencing research spans a spectrum from purely descriptive/correlational (sequence complexity vs. ERA/FIP) to fully causal (propensity-score stratification, counterfactual Transformer simulation), and the most recent (2025-2026) work leans toward deep-learning sequence models (Transformers) requiring full Statcast pitch-by-pitch logs, not aggregate stats.
- All sequencing papers found require pitch-level tracking data (PITCHf/x pre-2015, Statcast post-2015) at minimum including pitch type, location/zone, and outcome per pitch; none of them can be approximated from box-score or FanGraphs-leaderboard data alone.
- The reported effect sizes (up to >1.0 K/9 shift from optimized sequencing in the counterfactual paper) suggest sequencing effects are non-trivial but the literature is young/not yet consensus-validated — the counterfactual-impact paper is very recent (2026) and not yet reflected in secondary literature.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch full text of the MDPI Sports paper (403 Forbidden) or the JQAS propensity-score paper (binary/corrupted PDF retrieval) — findings for these two are drawn from search-engine synopses rather than verified primary-source reading; treat the specific numbers (74.5% predictability) as moderately but not fully confirmed.
- No quantitative out-of-sample validation (e.g., holdout-season RMSE against actual ERA) was found for the sequencing-complexity index or the graph-embedding method; these are described as finding correlational/structural evidence rather than being validated as standalone predictive run-prevention metrics the way FIP/SIERA/DRA are.

## For each method, how does it differ from the project's current baseline (single starting-pitcher Elo adjustment + Marcel-based xFIP projection, no Statcast ingestion)?

### Takeaway
Every FIP-family/SIERA-style estimator is a small addition (formula over existing FanGraphs-style rate stats, no new data pipeline), DRA/cFIP-style mixed-model estimators are a moderate addition (need play-by-play participant/context data — batter, catcher, umpire, park — beyond simple rate stats but still no pitch tracking), while Stuff+/Location+/Pitching+ models and all pitch-sequencing models (graph embeddings, Transformers, propensity-score causal analysis) are large architectural changes requiring net-new ingestion of raw Statcast pitch-level data (velocity, spin, movement, release point, location, pitch type per pitch) that the project does not currently have.

### Cited Findings
- SIERA requires only strikeout/walk/ground-ball/fly-ball rates per plate appearance — data "FanGraphs and MLB Gameday provide" — explicitly contrasted with pitch-level data needs. — [Introducing SIERA: Part 1](https://www.baseballprospectus.com/news/article/10027/introducing-siera-part-1/)
- DRA/cFIP's mixed-model approach needs play participant identities (batter, catcher, umpire) and park/platoon/role context per plate appearance, which is a step up from leaderboard rate stats but still not raw pitch-tracking data. — [An Updated Evaluation of Hitting and Pitching (Including Stuff) Metrics](https://www.baseballprospectus.com/news/article/82426/prospectus-feature-an-updated-evaluation-of-hitting-and-pitching-including-stuff-metrics/)
- Stuff+/Location+/Pitching+ and the sequencing papers all explicitly require pitch-level Statcast (or PITCHf/x) fields: release point, velocity, movement, spin rate/axis, location/zone, pitch type, and count, per pitch thrown. — [Stuff+, Location+, and Pitching+ Primer](https://library.fangraphs.com/pitching/stuff-location-and-pitching-primer/); [Decoding MLB Pitch Sequencing Strategies via Directed Graph Embeddings](https://www.sloansportsconference.com/research-papers/decoding-mlb-pitch-sequencing-strategies-via-directed-graph-embeddings)

### Inferences
- Relative to the project's current baseline (Elo + Marcel/xFIP, no pitch tracking): adopting SIERA or a DRA/cFIP-style context-adjusted estimator as an additional leaderboard/projection input would be a comparatively small-to-moderate lift (SIERA = pure formula swap; DRA/cFIP = needs participant/context-level play-by-play, which may already partially exist if play-by-play is ingested for other purposes, but needs mixed-model fitting infrastructure).
- Adopting any Stuff+-family pitch-quality metric or any pitch-sequencing model (graph embedding, Transformer-based counterfactual, propensity-score causal) would be a large architectural change: it requires standing up Statcast pitch-level ingestion (which the system explicitly lacks today), plus either licensing/training a run-value or outcome-classification model (Stuff+-style) or a sequence model (Transformer/graph-embedding-style) — substantially more data-engineering and ML-infrastructure investment than a leaderboard-metric swap.
- A middle-ground option not found as a single named paper but implied by the comparison data: using a published, replicable formula (SIERA/xFIP-level effort) while being aware that DRA/cFIP-style and Stuff+-style methods report meaningfully higher year-ahead predictive correlation (0.25-0.26 vs 0.18-0.19 in the one benchmark found) — so if higher predictive accuracy is a priority, the project should recognize that even the "cheap" SIERA-style upgrade leaves real predictive power on the table relative to context-adjusted or Statcast pitch-quality approaches.

### Gaps
- Could not determine whether the project's existing data ingestion already captures per-plate-appearance participant/context fields (batter, catcher, umpire, park) that a DRA/cFIP-style mixed model would need — this assessment is about the external method's general requirements, not an audit of the project's current schema (outside this research's scope, as it wasn't a web-search task).
